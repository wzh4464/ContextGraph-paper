Coding agents can pass a task's tests while overlooking requirements established by earlier repairs. We study how repair memory can make these omitted requirements actionable in a new task. ContextGraph represents each experience as a behavioral requirement, its triggering input, and an executable observation. Similarity retrieval selects candidate experiences, while graph relations connect historical input conditions to the current workflow to construct executable checks. In three paired Django development cases, memory-informed repairs recover omitted requirements even though both arms pass official verification. A further controlled comparison exposes a repair that satisfies the current and historical examples separately but fails when their conditions are combined; supplying the composed checks and execution feedback guides its correction. These mechanism studies complement separate system-level results on SWE-bench Verified, where ContextGraph resolves 346 of 500 tasks compared with 309 without memory, a gain of 7.4 percentage points. Together, the results motivate a concrete role for repair memory: carrying forward the conditions under which past requirements must still hold, and making their interaction with a new task available to execution and refinement.
